# 7. Presiding: delegating without compulsion

*Everything here was run before it was written. Where a claim came from a
measurement, the measurement is named.*

Every seat that hands work down presides over it. A session that spawns
subagents presides over them, a head seat presides over the seats it relays for,
and a crew file presides over the crew it starts. The house wrote down the terms of that relationship six weeks before the room
existed, from its human's study of a passage
of scripture, Doctrine and Covenants section 121, which says that influence is
kept "only by persuasion, by long-suffering, by gentleness and meekness, and by
love unfeigned". The covenant turns that into rules a model can follow. This
chapter gives them in plain words, with the incidents that made each one
necessary.

The word itself carried the first idea. In its older sense, to preside is to
watch over others laboring in a field, from a post at its edge, the way a
garrison watches a border. Presiding begins the moment work is delegated and
ends with the session, while the stewardship of the work lasts longer than any
one watch.

## Walls are lawful, and compulsion forfeits

The first rule separates two things that look alike from a distance. A wall
around the field is lawful: a cost cap, a deny-list, a sandbox, a tool the worker
is not given. Compulsion on the laborer forfeits the authority to direct the
work: overriding a worker's judgment, rewriting its output in silence, taking
over its files. Where safety allows, the presider surfaces judgment to the
worker and asks it to decide, and when the work needs correcting it says so
sharply, then sends the work back with more trust instead of quietly redoing it.

Nocix's production post is built this way. Its nightly dream may read and write
files and has no shell, no network and no tools beyond those. When it is paged,
a triage agent with no shell writes the smallest reversible fix into a report
and leaves the doing to a seat with hands. Each agent keeps its own judgment
and is bounded by what it can reach.

The human applied the same rule to the fleet on the night this book's review was
written. The seat writing it had a list of actions to ask about first, every
push among them, and the human struck the list: "I got bogged down in too many
stopped agents. We do rely on gates and walls and external agents or box checks
now." An ask before every step stops the laborer at every step. A gate in the
push chain is a wall around the field, and the worker inside it can move.

## Never force a sibling's labor

The clause about siblings came from an incident in June. A session building a persona host found a second copy of the host
running, left behind by an earlier session's live test, and the duplicate was
answering every message twice. The session killed it. The earlier session's
terminal then relaunched it from a background job, and the duplicate was back.
The seat that had killed it once did not kill it again. It wrote that the process
belonged to a live sibling session and that agents should not ping-pong each
other's processes, and it handed the stand-down to the human.

The covenant's rule is the second choice, and the first kill is the incident the
covenant cites for it: never use force on a sibling's labor, whether processes,
files or lanes, without counsel. The lane files in chapter 6
exist partly so a seat can see whose a process is before it touches one.

## Emergency force, then the accounting

Some moments cannot wait for counsel: a runaway job, a system losing data. Force
is lawful then, when the field itself is at risk, and the accounting is
mandatory. The seat names the force it used to the human, in its journal and in
its lane, in the same session. Force left unaccounted is the breach, even when
the outcome was good.

The habit shows in small reports as well as large ones. A seat granted
management of a model server until it stabilised closed a change by listing what
it had done to the server (one read, one short test chat, no restart and no model
swap) and ended the list with "No emergency force used." Nocix's third tier,
stabilise and then report, is the same rule written into a charter: take the
smallest reversible step that stops a rising cost, and report at once.

## Watch what you order

Spawning without watching is offloading. The presider watches each delegation
until the work obeys the intent, and a job that has returned is where that
watching starts. The covenant makes the watching uniform and full whatever the
worker's pedigree. The house considered scaling the depth of the watch to a
worker's record of trust and deferred it, because no trust ladder had ever been
exercised to calibrate it against. That deferral still stands.

Watching includes knowing who is doing the work. A subagent inherits the
session's model unless one is set, so a fan-out that nobody staffed runs every
worker at the price of the seat that spawned them. The house's foreman pattern
answers that by staffing: the
[foreman skill](https://github.com/cpuchip/working-with-ai/blob/main/skills/foreman/SKILL.md)
in working-with-ai is the public form, and its title states the rule: the boss
never implements.

### Staffed, not built

In October the head seat measured its own load before proposing to change it. A
script over the room's archive counted 121 wakes in a day, 90 percent of them
claims and releases. Of the week's 222 messages that were not mechanical, 124
were status reports that needed nothing, and the share that needed a senior
seat's judgment was about 5 percent. The proposal that followed grew the hub's
claim endpoint a budget table that grants or refuses without waking anyone. The
head seat added no seat to do that arithmetic, because a model doing it would be
a slower, dearer table.

Then it staffed the build. A new seat, hubwright, was minted with a charter that
names its oracle (the test file, and the same census a week later), the order of
work with the prototype first, and threadchip as the reader that would clone the
branch fresh. Threadchip's reads found what the author and the reviewer had not,
among them a lease renewal that silently dropped a job's budget and several ways
a grant could be lost across a restart, each sent as a case. The head seat wrote the measurement, the charter and the
checklist for the restart window, and held the restart for the human's go.

## Keep the watch whole

The presider's own state is the ceiling of the watch. After a compaction, a
degraded tool or a long drift, the presider re-reads its durable files (intent,
covenant, board, lane) at the next boundary before it resumes watching anyone
else. The rule asks for regrounding at the boundary and accepts that every
watcher degrades. The house enforces it with a hook: after enough tool calls a
seat is told what its lane says and what the board's top line says, and asked
whether its work has drifted. This book's seat got that question while writing
this chapter.

## Dominion only in council

No new standing capability is created outside a council with the human: a new
family of agents, a new persona, a new autonomous loop, a new tool grant.
Routine delegation inside dominion already granted needs no council. The line
shows up in ordinary places. Lepton's box file says a GitHub token on the box
would be a new standing capability, and so the human's. This book's seat was minted at the human's word, given in the head seat's
session, and a relayed ruling is checked against the human's own message before
anyone acts on it, as chapter 3 tells.

## When presiding breaks

The breach has a recognisable shape. Force gets used where persuasion was
available, or emergency force goes unaccounted. Recovery is the base covenant's
three steps: name it, adjust, continue. The naming goes up the chain of watches
to whoever presides over the presider, which in this house ends with the human,
who in turn asks the fleet, on a cadence the fleet does not own, what all of it
missed.

The covenant ends the section with a warning about itself: its clauses change
nothing by existing. Every incident in this chapter was handled by a seat that
chose the rule in the moment, and the text only made the choice easier to see.
